Strategic Verdict & Final AM4 Critique

Platform & Motherboard (Excellent): The ASUS ROG Strix X570-E is the perfect board for this. It explicitly supports x8/x8 bifurcation and has a **3-slot physical gap** between the primary and secondary x16 slots, meaning your two Acer Nitro 7800 XTs (2.5 slots thick) will fit perfectly without choking.

The 5900XT Pivot: You have deliberately overridden the value-focused 65W Ryzen 5600 in favor of the massive 16-core Ryzen 9 5900XT for €335. While the extra cores will sit mostly idle during pure GPU AI inference, this makes the machine a much stronger hybrid workstation for CPU-bound tasks (like rendering or compiling). Consequently, the free stock cooler has been dropped and replaced with the excellent Thermalright Peerless Assassin 120 SE.

Negotiated Deals: Instead of relying entirely on automated scraping, you successfully negotiated the Acer Nitro RX 7800 XT down to €400 on Adverts.ie and secured 64GB of DDR4 for €200 via Facebook Marketplace. These manual hustles keep the budget in check despite the CPU upgrade.

PSU Optimization: Despite the upgrade to the 105W 5900XT, an 850W ATX 3.1 PSU (like the Corsair RM850e or Fortron VITA) is still perfectly adequate. Dual 7800 XTs plus the 5900XT will draw ~700W sustained, and the ATX 3.1 spec handles the transient spikes. Therefore, the 1200W unit is still not needed.

The Intel Alternative (Why AM4 Wins): An Intel equivalent would use a cheap CPU like the Core i5-12400F (~€125). However, the massive roadblock is the motherboard. Finding an LGA1700 motherboard that supports proper x8/x8 PCIe lane bifurcation with a 3-slot physical gap is incredibly expensive. You are forced into premium "Creator" boards (like the ASUS ProArt Z790-Creator at ~€500) because standard gaming boards often steal GPU lanes if you plug in an M.2 drive. Furthermore, Z790 boards require expensive DDR5 RAM. The sheer availability of used, flagship X570 boards makes AMD the undisputed king of budget multi-GPU builds.

Gaming & VR Performance: If you plan to game, the AM4 build violently outclasses the GMKtec. While modern games cannot use both GPUs (SLI/Crossfire is dead), a single RX 7800 XT is roughly 80% to 90% faster than the Radeon 8060S iGPU found in the €1,850 GMKtec EVO-X2. The GMKtec's iGPU is impressive for its size (roughly matching a desktop RX 7600 XT), but it cannot push 1440p Ultra. For VR: The 7800 XT's dedicated GDDR6 VRAM provides the ultra-low latency necessary for a smooth, nausea-free 90Hz/120Hz PCVR experience. The GMKtec's shared UMA memory architecture, while brilliant for AI, introduces micro-stutters and frame latency spikes that make it a poor choice for serious VR gaming.

Final Verdict: By leveraging manual negotiations and actively choosing to build a stronger CPU hybrid workstation, your Phase 1 cost is set at **~€1,531.76**. The dual-GPU path remains the performance king.

The UMA Architecture Advantage (Why AI MAX keeps up):

You might wonder how an APU like the AI MAX 365+ (Strix Halo) with ~273 GB/s theoretical memory bandwidth keeps pace with a Dual RX 7800 XT setup (which boasts 624 GB/s per card).

The secret is Unified Memory Architecture (UMA) vs. PCIe Bottlenecks. When running large models (like 32B) that exceed the 16GB VRAM of a single consumer GPU, inference engines (like llama.cpp) must split the model layers across two cards. The two GPUs must constantly communicate intermediate results over the motherboard's PCIe bus. Consumer motherboards lack enterprise NVLink, so this communication incurs a massive latency penalty, dragging the 7800 XT and 4060 Ti down from their theoretical maximums.

The AI MAX 365+ features a massive 256-bit memory bus directly feeding 64GB of unified LPDDR5X-8533 RAM. Because the GPU and CPU share the same giant pool of memory, there is zero PCIe data transfer overhead and no layer-splitting latency. It leverages its full ~230 GB/s of effective bandwidth with absolute efficiency, allowing it to beat the dual 4060 Ti setup and trail just behind the dual 7800 XT setup, while costing nearly half the price.
